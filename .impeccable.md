## Design Context

### Users
Technical peers, collaborators, and hiring managers evaluating depth of work — readers coming from an engineering mindset who want signal fast: what this person builds, how they think, and whether it's worth a follow-up conversation. Site tagline: "From 'this might work' to running on a server." Voice: builder/experimenter, not corporate — personal, dry, ships things. Content is analyses, architecture write-ups, build logs, and notes — dense, technical, occasionally math/diagram-heavy (KaTeX, Mermaid already wired in).

### Brand Personality
Precise, dry, quietly confident. One deliberate flourish (the gold highlighter-mark on the name) against an otherwise restrained, editorial system — the opposite of a "look at all my colors" personal site. Voice reads like a technical field notebook, not a portfolio pitch.

### Aesthetic Direction
Pearl-white + gold-accent editorial system already established — warm off-white ground, near-white surfaces, charcoal ink, gold reserved for the accent; the original dark-navy palette survives behind the theme toggle. (Newsreader serif for display/headings, Inter for body, mono for eyebrows/metadata/tags.) Reference points: **thorstenball.com** (dense, text-first, minimal chrome, monospace-flavored, no card grids) and **lilianweng.github.io** (academic-blog clarity, generous whitespace around long-form technical content, clean TOC/heading hierarchy). Anti-reference: generic "AI startup" marketing-site tropes — hero gradients, stat tiles, icon+heading+text card grids repeated per section.

### Design Principles
1. **Content is the layout.** Favor long-form text rhythm (headings, whitespace, dividers) over cards/containers; reserve `.post-card`-style containers for genuinely distinct, clickable list items — never nest or stack them as decoration.
2. **One accent, used sparingly.** Gold marks the single most important thing per view (name, active state, hover). Don't spread accent color across multiple simultaneous elements.
3. **Mono for metadata, serif for meaning.** JetBrains Mono stays reserved for eyebrows/dates/tags; Newsreader serif for anything that's a title or a claim. Don't blend the two roles.
4. **Density matches medium.** Feed/index views can be denser (tight list rhythm like thorstenball.com); article bodies get generous line-length and vertical breathing room (like lilianweng.github.io).
5. **Left-aligned, asymmetric, no faux-marketing centering.** Avoid centered hero blocks, icon-grid feature sections, or symmetric card walls — those read as generic SaaS, not engineering notebook.
